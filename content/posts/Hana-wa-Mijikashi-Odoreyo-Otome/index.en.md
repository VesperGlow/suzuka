I first heard of this game while browsing a gaming community app. I've forgotten who posted about it and what exactly they said—only that they mentioned there was a very beautiful CG with the heroine, Chris. I happened to have a soft spot for cross-dressing stories at the time, so I took the plunge.

![The hero and heroine meet for the first time](001.webp)

This is the scene where the two leads first meet. The hero, Yamashina Rin, is the son of a celebrated kabuki *onnagata*—a male actor of female roles—and naturally inherited his father's craft. Skilled as he is in that art, he nonetheless suffers a serious setback on stage one day, and runs all the way to this lakeside, where he meets the heroine, Chris. Chris is an exchange student from England, born into an old and distinguished noble house; her full name is Christina White—a blonde tsundere loli, and I fell for her instantly. After finishing the game I also developed a keen interest in her voice actress, Kitami Rikka.

This is ensemble's 28th project, part of their long-running "Otome series"—stories about a hero who infiltrates a girls' school in disguise. The stage is set in the tenth year of the Taishō era, roughly a century ago: an age when kimono and Western dress, schoolgirls and noble ladies, all coexisted in the same frame. The *onnagata* of kabuki is already a man performing an idealized femininity, and this game simply pushes that premise to its limit: a man whose profession is to play women goes and becomes a woman for real.

![Chris at the lakeside](002.webp)

"Yes—it was a kabuki performance. But that ended long ago. So I've been wondering what to do next." When Chris said this at the lake, I hadn't yet grasped its weight. Only much later would I learn what that "kabuki performance, ended long ago" meant to her.

## A Man, Disguised as a Maiden, Slipping into a Girls' School

Roused by a few words from Chris after his performance fell apart, Yamashina Rin decides to enroll at the girls' school under the assumed name "Fujinami Rin," hoping to refine his art as an *onnagata*. Waiting for him is the "Eikakai," an elite group representing the whole student body, and an election to choose the "Nadeshiko no Kimi"—the single maiden at the summit of the school—from among the Eikakai's five members.

The headmistress positions our hero as the lead candidate of the traditionalist faction, set squarely against the lead candidate of the Westernizing faction: Chris. From the very start, then, their relationship is nailed into an awkward spot—the person he has to defeat is precisely the person he cares about most.

![In the election, Chris's support is overwhelming](003.webp)

"Chris's support is overwhelmingly high right now; I can't even compare myself to her. Honestly, I have no idea how I could possibly win against her."

Chris is the kind of person born to stand in the spotlight. Petite as she is, she's described as "small, yet magnificent and brilliant as golden light." She's accomplished at speed-reading and Western swordsmanship, and carries herself with a knightly gallantry that has the Westernizing girls swooning over her. She dislikes chopsticks, dislikes relying on others, and dislikes men—and yet, toward Rin alone, her pride carries a hint of something she can't quite name.

## Her Days at School, and the Cuteness She Can't Hide

The election aside, what really drew me in was Chris herself. She's a textbook tsundere, forever putting on an unyielding front toward Rin—but the longer they spend together, the more cracks show beneath that proud armor.

![Teased by her Eikakai companions until her face goes red](004.webp)

"E-enough! You don't have to say that out loud!"

Dressed in kimono at school and teased to her face by her Eikakai companions, she panics in an instant—worlds apart from the dashing "golden prince" on stage. It's in these little gaps of everyday life that I gradually understood: beneath that armor lives a girl who blushes easily, and who very much wants to be truly seen.

## When We Became Lovers, She Still Thought We Were Both Girls

The most interesting thing about this route is that it first lets the two fall in love "as maidens." Chris takes "Fujinami Rin" for a fellow girl the whole time—the person she's drawn to, grows close to, and finally commits her heart to is someone she believes is also a girl.

![The first kiss](005.webp)

This is the very CG that got me into the game. Dark-haired Rin (in his female guise) and golden-haired Chris are entwined together; in the blue glow, their long hair spreads out like a tide. Honestly, this single image alone made the whole thing worth it.

![“Chris always throws herself at me with everything she has”](006.webp)

"Chris always throws herself at me with everything she has, and even at a time like this, that didn't change." She does everything at full tilt—even sweetness and closeness come without a shred of holding back.

![Bashful, but refusing to back down](007.webp)

"…Though honestly, I don't really know what lovers are supposed to do between two girls."

That line lays bare the color of their relationship right then: in Chris's heart, this is a romance "between two maidens." She's nervous, clumsy, utterly clueless about how to be with "another girl"—and yet deadly earnest about it.

From Rin's side, this sweetness is always strung taut by a single wire—he knows perfectly well he is hiding a secret that cannot be kept forever. The closer the two grow, the tighter that wire pulls.

## A Secret That Couldn't Stay Hidden

No matter how well it's hidden, this disguise was never going to last. After they become lovers, Rin notices a small locket hanging at Chris's neck, holding a single photograph. It is, she says, of someone immensely important to her—someone who once gave her strength.

That night, Rin is alone in the bath, lost in thought about that locket, when Chris suddenly bursts in—afraid he might be jealous over it, anxious to explain herself. But the moment the door opens, the male body Rin has hidden for so long falls bare into her sight. The two stand speechless; Rin hurriedly throws on his clothes and flees back to his room, unable for the life of him to think of how to begin explaining, certain that everything is over.

## Afraid of Men as She Was, She Still Kept Him

To understand the weight of this moment, you first have to know why Chris fears men so much. Her father—a diplomat posted to this country and a leader of its Westernizing reforms—only ever praised her older brother. No matter how desperately Chris excelled at swordsmanship and horsemanship, leaving her brother far behind, in her father's eyes "a woman shouldn't do anything unnecessary," and her efforts never earned her a single word of recognition. Her fixation on becoming Nadeshiko no Kimi began, at its root, as a wish to win her father's acknowledgment. That a girl rebuffed again and again by her father would come to regard "men" as a whole with wariness is only natural.

And yet fate played a not-so-small joke on her—the very person she'd tried so hard to draw close to, the one she finally chose, turned out to be a man. Anyone else might well have turned and fled the instant the truth came out. But Chris didn't. She came after him, without the slightest disdain, and instead quietly asked his real name. What she fears is the abstract category of "men"; what she has chosen is this one specific person. By all rights it should have ended there—and it was she who let the story go on.

## We Had Met Long, Long Ago

The real name she asked for uncovered the most important card of this route: Rin and Chris did not meet for the first time at that lake.

![Chris as a child](008.webp)

"I'm… Chris."

Rin's stage name is Yamashina Ichika. And the photograph Chris has treasured in that locket all her life is of this very "Ichika." Many years ago, the child Chris watched a kabuki performance, and the figure she took for a "prince" on that stage was the young Rin. A boy whose trade was to play women, who looked to that foreign little girl like a prince through and through—that contrast in itself is one of this game's finest touches.

![“Ichika, just like a prince…!”](009.webp)

![The promise the young Rin made](010.webp)

"When I grow up, you have to come see me again. By then I'll have become beautiful enough not to lose to you—just you wait."

After that performance, the young Ichika met the doll-like Chris. He told her how much he loved kabuki, and how little he cared for the gossip of others—and it was precisely that openness, that ability to "truly be himself," that moved her so deeply. Before leaving, he even teased her: "When we meet again, marry me—by then I'll have become even prettier than you." That one line, "you're so terribly pretty and cute," Chris carried with her all the way to today.

![“So Chris and I… we actually met a long, long time ago.”](011.webp)

So that "kabuki performance, ended long ago" in Chris's heart was the first meeting she could never forget, even across an ocean; and the girl who competed so fiercely at this school was, at her core, still a little girl longing to see "Ichika" again. When Rin pieced it all together, that sense of fate—"we exchanged a promise back when we were both so small"—recolored every clash that had come before. From the very beginning, they were never rivals.

## What She Loves Is Me with the Disguise Stripped Away

What truly moved me about this route is not the "the hero is actually a man" twist itself, but Chris's reaction once she knew the truth.

![“You're the one who showed me the light”](012.webp)

"When I was crying all alone in the pitch dark, you're the one who showed me the light. You're the most important person to me… the person I love most. To me, that's who you are."

When she fell for "Fujinami Rin," she thought he was a girl; now that she knows Rin is a man, knows he is the very "Ichika" of long ago, the person she loves hasn't changed in the slightest. The one who first pulled her out of the dark, and the one beside her now, were the same person all along—Fujinami Rin, Yamashina Rin, Ichika: three names, one soul. What Chris loves was never any single identity.

![“You simply look at the real me”](013.webp)

"…And that's why I love you most of all. You don't care about things like feminine charm… you simply look at the real me."

I stared at this line for a long time. The whole game plays with these stacked-up disguises—the *onnagata*, the cross-dressing, the idealized woman—yet at the heart of the feeling, what both of them want is something utterly plain: to be loved by someone who doesn't care whether you're "womanly enough," who simply looks at the real you. Chris has always been treated as that flawless "golden prince," and Rin let her set that halo down; Rin has spent half his life "playing someone else," and Chris was willing to see past every disguise and recognize only the real him. Two people who had lived too long inside a performance finally took off the costume in front of each other.

## "Marry Me"—the Truth That Slipped Out

One night near the story's end, Chris has fallen asleep against Rin. Gazing at her sleeping face, he finally pieces back together the childhood memory he'd forgotten for far too long—the figure in his dream really was Chris.

![Replay of the proposal](014.webp)

"Ah, what an idiot I am…! A fool…! How could I have forgotten something so important?" In his frustration, he mutters, almost to himself, "…Marry me, or something."

He thought Chris was asleep. He didn't expect—

![“I-I will…!!”](015.webp)

"I-I will…!!"

Chris, who should have been fast asleep, springs up and answers fast and breathless. A half-joking, half-earnest murmur he never meant to be heard was caught, squarely, by her. And that "marry me" connects right back to the very same promise the young Ichika made her all those years ago—back then he was the one who said it first, and now, having forgotten and remembered again, it's still him who says it first. One makes a proposal sound like he's scolding himself for being forgetful; the other shouts her answer like she's buzzing in first on a quiz. It's these two, all over, exactly as they've always been.

## On Stage, Speaking the Truth as Lines

Their relationship can't be hidden any longer, and the students simply decide, as one, that the two of them should be Nadeshiko no Kimi together. But that would break the rules, so the teachers hit upon a compromise—let Rin and Chris settle it with a stage play. They perform *Romeo and Juliet*: Chris as Romeo, Rin as Juliet, two lovers forced to part—a perfect mirror of the school's own situation in that moment.

![An embrace](016.webp)

On stage the two cross blades, and after who knows how long, there's a sharp *crack*—both swords shatter at once. Seizing the gap, Rin pulls Chris in and kisses her before the entire audience. The hall erupts in screams—this play, half-real and half-acted for so long, lands at last, at the center of the stage, back on something true.

Among the audience sit two people watching with particular intensity—Headmistress Chinami, and the special-appointment teacher Spark. As it happens, Spark too is a man hiding under a disguise, just like Rin; he and Chinami had once been kept apart by the very same divide of tradition and the West, and years ago even tried to elope, only to fail—after which he stayed disguised for years on end, just to remain at her side. Watching this pair finally cross the very threshold the two of them never could, the old headmistress only sighs: just this once, then. This quietly buried subplot is almost a mirror of Rin and Chris—the same cross-dressing, the same wall of the times standing between them—except the previous generation lost to that threshold, and this time, the young ones won it back.

![“Until the very moment this body perishes, I'll always be by your side”](017.webp)

"—Yes. Until the very moment this body perishes, I'll always be by your side…"

The proud noble lady who once hated relying on anyone, who had to tough out everything, in the end handed her whole self over. Afterward, the Nadeshiko no Kimi election ended without a winner—too many ballots either bore both their names or were left blank—so the school simply declared Rin and Chris the joint first Nadeshiko no Kimi. As they clasped each other's hands on the podium, Chris had long since stopped clinging to her father's approval: the person she loves most, and most wants approval from, was standing right beside her.

## Brief as a Bloom, and So All the More to Dance

Looking back at the title after finishing—*Hana wa Mijikashi, Odoreyo Otome*, "the blossom is brief, so dance, maiden"—I find it beautifully chosen. A kabuki performance comes to its close; the girls'-school days of one's maidenhood will end; and along with them, that gorgeous female costume and that golden prince must one day take their final bow. Precisely because the blossom is so brief, all the more reason to dance with all your heart while it's still in bloom—just as Rin and Chris did, in the end, on that stage.

I came for the novelty of the cross-dressing premise and that one beautiful CG, and was unexpectedly struck by the conviction at the heart of this route: "no matter which layer of identity you wear, the one I love is the real you." Chris first thought she'd fallen for a girl, then found out he was a man—the very "prince" she had looked up to as a child—and yet her heart never wavered from beginning to end. For a hero who has taken "playing someone else" as his fate, to meet someone willing to see through every disguise and recognize only the real him is, perhaps, the most lasting thing this story—brief as a blossom—leaves him with.
